\# Sauce \& Spoon: Menu Tablet Rollout



A project management capstone: planning, piloting, launching and closing out a menu-tablet rollout for a fictional restaurant chain, Sauce \& Spoon. The project is documented end to end, from the project charter to the impact report for senior stakeholders.



> Sauce \& Spoon is a fictional company created for a course exercise. The documents are my own work on that scenario.



\## The project



Sauce \& Spoon's sales were growing, but slower service was leading to guest complaints and flat profit margins. The project piloted tabletop menu tablets at two locations (North and Downtown) so guests could order and pay at the table, speeding up service and giving the team clear data to decide on a wider rollout.



\*\*My role:\*\* project manager (Peta), from the charter to the closeout.

\## Challenges



\- \*\*Stakeholder misalignment.\*\* Managers wanted different things from the pilot: order-return and meal-replacement policies, a kitchen staff satisfaction goal, a 20% appetizer sales target, and moving front-of-house payroll to hire kitchen staff. I recorded each one in the charter with the decision taken and what was still open, so that the goals stayed measurable and in scope.

\- \*\*Goals that couldn't be measured yet.\*\* Several targets had no baseline, so they were left as TBD until data was collected, rather than inventing numbers.

\- \*\*The test launch didn't hit every goal.\*\* Table turn time barely moved, about 28% of orders were incorrect, 12% of guests had technical issues, and satisfaction was 72%.

\- \*\*Operational surprises.\*\* Cash payers had a slower, more frustrating checkout. Implementation ran longer than planned because staff vacation time wasn't accounted for. Each location also had internal operational issues that weren't in the plan.



\## Outcomes



After acting on the test-launch feedback (a simpler navigation layout, clearer payment messaging, a pre-service testing checklist, and training on guest pacing), the tablets launched on April 23 with these results:



| Goal | Result |

|---|---|

| Reduce table turn time | Down 30 minutes |

| Reduce food waste by 25% | Reached 25% |

| Increase daily guest count by 10% | Up 20% at Downtown |

| Customer satisfaction | From 72% (pilot) to 86% (launch) |

| Technical issues below 5% weekly | Met |

| Tablet checkout in one minute or less | Met |

| Monthly revenue | From $65,000 in April to $78,000 in July, about +20% |



\*\*Cost:\*\* planned $50,550 against $15,914 actual, because the tablet hardware and software came as an annual subscription ($3,600) rather than a one-time purchase.



\## Lessons learned



\- Learn each location's operations and team availability before planning.

\- Run a test launch, a survey and a retrospective at every new location.

\- Build staff training on guest pacing into the plan, since the tablets alone did not speed up tables.

\- Keep the weekly vendor calls, which kept tasks and dependencies clear.

\- Align on objectives and how to measure them before the project starts.
